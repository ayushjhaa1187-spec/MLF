The orthogonal projection matrix is $P = A(A^T A)^{-1}A^T = \text{diag}(1, 1, 0)$. Adjust observation vector $b = [b_x, b_y, b_z]^T$ below. Notice that the projection $p = Pb$ remains confined to the plane, while the residual error $e = b - p$ is strictly perpendicular to every column of $A$ ($a_1^T e = 0$ and $a_2^T e = 0$).

Interactive Lab • Week 6

SVD: 3-Stage Geometric Action & Low-Rank Approximation

Practice Week 6 Week 6 Textbook

Every matrix transformation $x \mapsto Ax$ factorizes into three elementary geometric steps: $A = U \Sigma V^T$. Step 1: $V^T$ rotates/reflects the input space. Step 2: $\Sigma$ stretches the circle into an ellipse with semi-axis lengths $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$. Step 3: $U$ rotates the ellipse in the output space. Use the phase buttons below to inspect the coordinate geometry at each stage and test rank truncation ($A_1 = \sigma_1 u_1 v_1^T$).

PCA: Maximizing Projected Variance & Reconstruction Error

Practice Week 7 Week 7 Textbook

Principal Component Analysis finds a projection direction $u$ that maximizes projected variance: $\max_{\|u\|=1} u^T \Sigma u = \lambda_1$. By the Pythagorean theorem: $\text{Projected Variance } V(\theta) + \text{Reconstruction MSE } E(\theta) = \text{Tr}(\Sigma) = 5.48$.
