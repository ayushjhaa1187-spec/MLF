Problem 1: Least Squares Orthogonal Projection
Linear Algebra (Week 3)

Given the matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and target vector $b = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$, find:

  1. The least squares solution $\hat{x}$ minimizing $\|Ax - b\|_2^2$.
  2. The projection vector $p$ of $b$ onto the column space $\text{Col}(A)$.
  3. Verify that the error vector $e = b - p$ is orthogonal to $\text{Col}(A)$.
Complete Solution:

Step 1: Compute $A^T A$ and $A^T b$:

$$A^T A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$A^T b = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Step 2: Solve Normal Equations $(A^T A)\hat{x} = A^T b$:

$$\hat{x} = (A^T A)^{-1} A^T b = \frac{1}{3} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1/3 \\ 1/3 \end{bmatrix}$$

Step 3: Projection $p$ and Error $e$:

$$p = A\hat{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 1/3 \\ 1/3 \\ 2/3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad e = b - p = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - 1/3 \\ 1 - 1/3 \\ 0 - 2/3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2/3 \\ 2/3 \\ -2/3 \end{bmatrix}$$

Orthogonality Verification: $A^T e = \begin{bmatrix} 2/3 - 2/3 \\ 2/3 - 2/3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$. Thus $e \perp \text{Col}(A)$.

Problem 2: PCA Variance Maximization via Rayleigh Quotient
Dimensionality Reduction (Week 7)

Let sample covariance matrix be $\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$. Find the first principal component direction $u_1$ and the percentage of total variance it explains.

Complete Solution:

Step 1: Characteristic equation $\det(\Sigma - \lambda I) = 0$:

$$(5 - \lambda)(2 - \lambda) - 4 = \lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 6 = (\lambda - 6)(\lambda - 1) = 0 \implies \lambda_1 = 6, \; \lambda_2 = 1$$

Step 2: Eigenvector for $\lambda_1 = 6$:

$$(\Sigma - 6I)v = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 2 & -4 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v_1 \\ v_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \implies -v_1 + 2v_2 = 0 \implies v = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Normalized unit vector: $u_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

Explained Variance: $\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1 + \lambda_2} = \frac{6}{6 + 1} = \frac{6}{7} \approx 85.71\%$.

Problem 3: Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) Optimality
Optimization & Duality (Week 10)

Solve the constrained optimization problem: $$\min_{x_1, x_2} (x_1 - 1)^2 + (x_2 - 2)^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad x_1 + x_2 \le 1, \; x_1 \ge 0, \; x_2 \ge 0$$

Complete Solution:

Unconstrained minimum is at $(1, 2)$, which violates $x_1 + x_2 \le 1$ ($1+2 = 3 > 1$). Thus the constraint $x_1 + x_2 = 1$ is active at optimum.

Lagrangian: $\mathcal{L}(x_1, x_2, \lambda) = (x_1 - 1)^2 + (x_2 - 2)^2 + \lambda (x_1 + x_2 - 1)$.

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x_1} = 2(x_1 - 1) + \lambda = 0 \implies x_1 = 1 - \frac{\lambda}{2}$$ $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x_2} = 2(x_2 - 2) + \lambda = 0 \implies x_2 = 2 - \frac{\lambda}{2}$$

Substitute into active constraint $x_1 + x_2 = 1$:

$$(1 - \lambda/2) + (2 - \lambda/2) = 1 \implies 3 - \lambda = 1 \implies \lambda = 2 > 0$$

Therefore: $x_1^* = 1 - 1 = 0$, and $x_2^* = 2 - 1 = 1$. The optimal point is $(0, 1)$ with minimum objective value $(0-1)^2 + (1-2)^2 = 2$.
